Zion's Black Wall Street

The empty land across from The Mall at Stonecrest becomes a district of light: original game shows, a late-night studio, a comedy club, a restaurant row, and a grand outdoor amphitheater. Built by Stonecrest, filled by our churches and creators, and owned by our community.

The land

For over twenty years, this ground has waited.

The lots directly in front of The Mall at Stonecrest along I-20 have sat empty for decades. Hotels went up with nothing to visit. Motor Mile grew up around a struggling mall. Families drive past every day on their way to spend their money somewhere else.

Zion's Black Wall Street turns that empty land into a destination. It honors the legacy of Tulsa's Greenwood District, burned in 1921, and declares Stonecrest the city that rebuilds it.

The district

Six anchors. One walkable plaza.

Watch a taping, eat dinner, catch a late show, and stay for a concert, all without moving the car.

Anchor

Game Show Hall

Two soundstages with live studio audiences of 300 to 500 each. Families come to watch, and families come to compete.

Anchor

Late-Night Studio

A broadcast-ready studio with a 250-seat audience and a band stage for a nightly talk and variety show. It doubles as a home for church media, interviews and podcasts.

Anchor

Comedy Club

A 300-seat room for clean, family-friendly comedy, open mics and touring headliners. A date night you can tell your pastor about.

Anchor

Restaurant Row

Six to ten restaurants, with first priority to Black-owned and Stonecrest-owned operators. Lunch for the Mall crowd, dinner before the show.

Anchor

Central Plaza

A fountain, an outdoor screen showing live tapings, and a stage for pop-up performances and seasonal markets. Free for everyone, every day.

Anchor

Grand Amphitheater

A huge open-air amphitheater at the rear of the complex. Concerts, gospel festivals, citywide worship nights, orchestra, comedy specials and the biggest live tapings.

Who owns it

The city builds the house. The community fills it.

We create our own wealth and lead by example. Ownership is shared so every partner reaps from the harvest, and the structure stays on firm legal ground.

The City of Stonecrest

Owns the venue

Through a public-private partnership, the city owns the land, studios, plaza and amphitheater, and earns from rent, bookings, parking and visitor spending for every resident.

Churches & creators

Own the shows

Church partners and Kingdom producers create the shows, keep the rights and share in the revenue from streaming, sponsorships and merchandise.

Local businesses

Run the district

Black-owned and Stonecrest-owned restaurants and operators get first priority for leases, so the jobs and profits stay in our neighborhoods.

The path

From vision to ribbon cutting

  1. Now – November 2027

    Share the vision

    Bring the plan to every pastor, church and family in DeKalb County and build the coalition.

  2. 2027

    Start the shows first

    Pilot the first original shows in partner churches and stream them online, proving the audience before a single wall goes up.

  3. First 100 days in office

    Study the site

    Commission a market and financing study for the land across from the Mall.

  4. Year one

    Form the partnership

    Select a development partner, bring church partners to the table, secure the land and win council approval.

  5. Years two to four

    Build and open

    Open the plaza and restaurants first, then the studios and comedy club, with the Grand Amphitheater as the capstone.
